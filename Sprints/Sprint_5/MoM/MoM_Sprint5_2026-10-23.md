## 2. Key Discussion Points

### A. Application demonstration
- Jaime demonstrated Compose, Library, Calendar, Inventory, Media, and Platforms, including Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, and a test platform.
- Posts can be created with captions, hashtags, images, post types, and a specific date or library-based scheduling. Library shows scheduled and waiting posts; Calendar shows posting slots and allows changes to a slot's type or content selection.
- During this demonstration, the Google Calendar connection was **not yet active**. The team reported a Google Workspace connection issue and planned to test with a separate developer Google account before integrating with the client's Workspace.

### B. Post rotation and calendar behavior
- The client requested more control over the posting sequence. For LinkedIn, **every other post should be a newsletter**, with the intervening posts alternating between About Us and External Article.
- Facebook and Instagram usually follow a simpler rotation, but holidays frequently require manual changes. The client asked whether changing one calendar slot could affect just that slot or the later rotation; supporting both behaviors was discussed as a possible enhancement.
- The client asked whether changing posting days would reschedule future events already added to Google Calendar. The team said Google Calendar behavior still needed testing.
- The client requested a conflict warning when a Specific Date post overlaps another post for the **same platform, date, and time**. The team agreed to add or verify this behavior.

### C. Inventory and media
- The Inventory page compares available posts with empty slots across the current and following month, and shows a 14-day upcoming schedule. The client requested clearer **month labels** so staff can tell which period the counts refer to.
- The Media page shows uploaded images, use counts, and platform suitability warnings. The client raised concerns about maintaining an image library over several years and requested a way to clean up unused media.
- The client discussed automatic image conversion to **JPEG** to reduce posting-format problems, rather than depending on staff to convert files manually. The implementation location (application versus existing publishing workflow) remains to be decided.

### D. Composer usability
- Ilana explained that staff usually include hashtags directly in the caption; the separate hashtag field may therefore be unnecessary.
- Robert requested that **alt text remain available but optional** because future accessibility requirements may call for it.
- The client requested that an **image become required**, with required fields visibly marked (for example, with an asterisk) and optional fields labeled.
- The client requested moving **Post Type** and **Color** higher in the form, directly after the platform selector.

### E. Deployment and access control
- The team described the React frontend, Node.js backend, SQLite storage, and Docker packaging, with a temporary Cloudflare tunnel being used for testing and demos. This was **not a confirmed deployment on the client's server**.
- Robert mentioned MariaDB as an available database on the client side; the transcript does not establish a decision to migrate from SQLite.
- Ilana estimated that approximately **four staff accounts** would use the scheduling system. Robert raised the need to prevent unauthorized users from modifying the calendar.
- Options discussed included application login, role-based permissions, and integrating access with the existing WordPress login; no final authentication approach was selected. Robert agreed to consider a secure solution, potentially involving validation through n8n.

## 3. Decisions and Agreements

1. **Keep alt text optional** in the composer; do not remove the field.
2. **Require an image** for posts and clearly mark required versus optional inputs.
3. **Move Post Type and Color** nearer the top of the composer, immediately after Platform.
4. **Document platform-specific rotation patterns**, including LinkedIn's alternating newsletter sequence, before implementing more complex rotation rules.
5. **Add clearer month identification** to the inventory display.
6. **Investigate automatic JPEG conversion** and long-term media cleanup; implementation approach remains open.
7. **Test Google Calendar synchronization** before client Workspace integration or claiming deployment completion.
8. **Investigate secure multi-user access**; authentication design has not yet been finalized.

*The items above reflect requests and discussion, not proof that each change was already implemented.*

## 5. Client Feedback / Clarifications

- The client confirmed that the system needs to accommodate holidays and changes to normal posting rotations without losing schedule flexibility.
- The client prefers staff-friendly upload handling so users do not need to remember image-format rules.
- The client wants a clear distinction between required and optional composer fields.
- Roughly four staff members may need access. Authentication should be secure and ideally avoid unnecessary repeat logins.
- Additional Google Calendar testing is necessary to confirm how future schedule changes affect existing calendar events.
